## 1. Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models
**Qizheng Zhang, Changran Hu, Shubhangi Upasani, Boyuan Ma, Fenglu Hong, Vamsidhar Kamanuru, Jay Rainton, Chen Wu, Mengmeng Ji, Hanchen Li, Urmish Thakker, James Zou, Kunle Olukotun** (Stanford / SambaNova / UC Berkeley) — **ICLR 2026**; arXiv:2510.04618 v1 6 Oct 2025, v3 29 Mar 2026 (32 pp.) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618

**Core mechanism.** Context is a *playbook* of structured, itemized bullets, each with a unique id plus counters for how often the Generator marked it helpful vs. harmful, and content holding one reusable strategy, domain concept, or failure mode. Three roles split the labor: Generator produces trajectories, Reflector distills concrete insights from successes and errors, Curator turns them into compact **delta** entries that a **non-LLM, deterministic merge** folds in — new ids are appended, existing bullets are updated in place (counter increments), and a **semantic-embedding de-duplication** step prunes redundancy. Refinement is grow-and-refine, triggered either proactively after each delta or lazily only when the context window is exceeded. The explicit target is the pair of failure modes named in the abstract: *brevity bias* (concise summaries drop domain insight) and *context collapse* (iterative full rewriting erodes detail over time).

**Evidence quality.** Strong. Offline (system prompt) and online (agent memory) adaptation across agent and finance benchmarks: +10.6% agents, +8.6% finance, up to +17.1% on AppWorld, where it matches the top-ranked production agent on overall average and beats it on the harder test-challenge split with a smaller open-source model. Ablations cover the Reflector, multi-epoch refinement, and the incremental-delta update individually; plus four backbones (GPT-OSS-120B, GPT-5.1, Llama-3.3-70B), reflection-quality robustness, and cost (80.8%/83.6% fewer input/output tokens than GEPA on AppWorld). Two cautions from its own ablations: the **deduplication threshold is non-monotone** (FiNER 70.7 base → 77.0 at 50%, 73.9 at 70%, 78.6 at 90%), and performance degrades if *every* update is adversarial.

**Transfer note.** Borrow the bullet schema and the deterministic non-LLM merge — an id per case with helpful/harmful counters is precisely a per-case confidence ledger, and delta-merge-by-id is the cheap, reviewable alternative to whole-file rewrites; but beware that ACE's usefulness signal (the Generator marking its own bullets helpful or harmful) is **self-reported**, the exact debt your `confidence.self_resolved` field already exists to quarantine.

## 2. Recursive Experiential–Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses (Recuris)
**Zhaochen Yu (NUS/Princeton), Yingcheng Wu (Stanford), Zhenfei Yin, Kaiyuan Chen (Oxford), Zhe Zhao (Stanford), Mengdi Wang (Princeton), Shuicheng Yan (NUS), Ling Yang (Princeton)** — arXiv preprint (cs.AI), **25 Aug 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24876 · code https://github.com/Gen-Verse/Recuris

**Core mechanism.** Memory is a four-component tuple `M = (E, W, ρ, C)`: experiential **skills in the agent-skill format** (E), the working-memory *specification* — state schema plus update proposal (W), the **invocation policy** (ρ, trigger predicate + retrieval key), and the checker set (C). Within a task, a compact working state whose goal entries carry `pending|done|blocked` plus supporting evidence is the retrieval key: skills are invoked at defined *execution events* (call-time, i.e. a drafted state-changing tool call is intercepted and not executed until the skill is in context; or boundary), not from the initial instruction or the full history. Checkers decide which proposed state changes the observation actually supports, and a fixed kernel commits only supported ones. Across tasks, a **fixed Meta-Agent** reads the structured trace `Γ` (state, invoked skills, action, observation, proposed state, checker decisions per step), attributes each failure to **one component**, and writes a component-scoped patch; a **fixed validation gate accepts only if the patch repairs the failed source task *and* does not regress a held-out development set of anchor tasks**.

**Evidence quality.** Strongest 2026 evidence base in this slice. Four long-horizon benchmarks (τ²-Bench, SkillFlow, Terminal-Bench 2.1), ten models from 3B to frontier, 35 of 37 completed model–benchmark pairs improved; +17.8 pts for GPT-5.6 Sol and +15.6 for Claude Opus 5 on τ²; +32.2 pts on the longest tasks; long-horizon failure modes down up to 80%. The single most transferable measurement: **component-level failure localization is 64.8% macro accuracy from the structured trace vs. 37.0% from the raw trajectory and 13.0% from the outcome alone** (33.3% constant-answer floor; macro precision 27.6% → 64.4%). Honest limits stated: one benchmark's checker class had to be excluded as decode noise; the gate is *finer-grained than it can resolve*; and their memory **only grows** — across eight accepted patches, 51 skills added, 2 revised, 0 deprecated, with **17 near-duplicate pairs surviving into admitted versions**, and ablating individual skills moved held-out results by only −2.3 to +1.7 pts.

**Transfer note.** This is the closest published analogue to your architecture (failure attribution → scoped card edit → held-out gate) and it validates the whole shape, but its admitted package carries 17 unpruned near-duplicates and no deprecation operator — your groom's pruning/dedup pass is the missing counterpart, and their localize-then-patch discipline implies your agent-proposed cards should **name the implicated field** rather than propose whole-file replacements.

## 3. APEX-EM: Non-Parametric Online Learning for Autonomous Agents via Structured Procedural-Episodic Experience Replay
**Pratyay Banerjee, Masud Moshtaghi, Ankit Chadha** (Adobe Research) — **EMNLP 2026** (accepted); arXiv:2603.29093 v1 31 Mar 2026, v3 31 Aug 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29093

**Core mechanism.** Complete procedural-episodic traces are stored in a typed **Procedural Knowledge Graph** and retrieved through **three parallel channels**: semantic search, structural-signature matching over abstract operation sequences, and graph traversal. A Plan-Retrieve-Generate-Iterate-Ingest (PRGII) workflow produces, **quality-gates**, and commits experiences, and it deliberately **indexes failures alongside successes** so the agent learns both what to reuse and what to avoid. No weights change at deployment.

**Evidence quality.** Strong and unusually well-controlled: five benchmarks (BigCodeBench, KGQAGen-10k, HLE, Lifelong Agent Bench, ALFWorld) with claims based on **same-backbone comparisons**. Held-out BigCodeBench transfer, shared GPT-4o: +7.6 pp over no-memory vs. 2.3× that (+2.3 pp) for MemRL under identical setup; frozen memory transfers to a blind 1,079-question KGQAGen-10k test split at 73.7% vs. 42.0% no-memory, approaching an 84.9% ground-truth-subgraph oracle; gains stay at +27 to +32 pp across three Opus scales, i.e. additive to model capability. Component analysis reports **structural signatures give 3.3× the transfer of semantic-only retrieval**, teacher feedback is negligible for code but +10.3 pp on structured queries, and within-epoch iteration recovers most of the gain when rich feedback is unavailable.

**Transfer note.** Direct support for two of your choices — lexical/structural matching over embeddings for transfer, and indexing negative cases — plus a warning that no single retrieval channel dominates, so tiering channels per domain and measuring which one earned the hit matters more than picking one.

## 4. From Faulty Memories to Corrected Actions: Dependency-Guided Rollback Repair for Memory-Augmented Agents
**Caili Yu, Yiqi Wang, Jiaqi Zhang, Yiqun Duan, Mingkai Zheng, Zhangkai Wu, Kaize Shi, Taotao Cai** (Univ. of Queensland / UTS / Adelaide) — arXiv preprint (cs.AI), **11 Aug 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10502

**Core mechanism.** Formalizes **post-failure memory recovery**: given a failed execution and diagnosed faulty memories, recover the answer *and* persistent state while retaining unaffected work. A **typed memory-to-action graph** is built from runtime provenance, explicit downstream dependencies are traced, candidates with independent trusted support are preserved, unsupported memory state is deactivated, and only answer-relevant affected computation is selectively replayed. The stated defect in prior defenses: deleting the source memory leaves already-propagated claims, actions, and derived memories active, while full reset or full replay destroys benign state and repeats computation.

**Evidence quality.** Medium. A 150-case controlled benchmark across three tool-use domains and four memory failure types (85.3% recovery vs. 77.3% best competitor; removes all diagnosed faulty memories and preserves all benign ones at modest LLM-call cost) plus a 50-case trajectory-derived stress test adapted from LongMemEval-V2 (68.0% vs. 54.0%; claim-invalidation F1 0.669 vs. 0.603). Scale is small and the benchmarks are mostly constructed rather than naturalistic; the authors state the results do not imply uniformly better trace reconstruction.

**Transfer note.** Your misdiagnosis feedback loop currently ends at a confidence decrement — this says a wrong card also leaves *derived* artifacts stale, so `related_references` / `ref_knowledge` one-hop edges are the propagation surface to check on a misdiagnosis, not just the offending case.

## 5. TEPA: Revoking Stale Memories for Conflict-Robust Language Agents
**Yan Zhou, Yue Ouyang, Kaiyang Zheng, Suncheng Xiang** — arXiv preprint (cs.AI), v1 7 Aug 2026, v2 10 Aug 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429

**Core mechanism.** Names the failure mode **memory pollution**: degradation caused by *active* memories that newer conflicting evidence has superseded. TEPA makes **validity an explicit state of memory**: observations are held as **keyed precedents**, and active precedents are **revoked** when fresh evidence contradicts them *under the same key*; retrieval draws only from current evidence while revoked history is preserved for audit. The decisive operation is current-key replacement, with revocation as a first-class lifecycle transition rather than deletion.

**Evidence quality.** Medium-strong for the specific claim, with a striking control. Over 50 seeds of controlled hidden-regime drift under full reversal, **append-only and last-write-wins both collapse to 0.210, below no-memory at 0.309, while TEPA reaches 0.950**; the pattern reproduces under real file-backed executable drift (0.203 / 0.298 / 0.950). On clean MemoryAgentBench SH-6k it merely *matches* a strong last-write-wins cache, which the authors read as confirming that current-key replacement is what matters for single-hop fact consolidation. Boundary tests on multi-hop and very-long-context settings expose remaining retrieval-chain and context-selection bottlenecks.

**Transfer note.** The most alarming result in this slice for your design: two of the three most natural revision policies scored **below having no knowledge base at all** once the world reversed — so staleness cannot be left to a decaying confidence score, it needs an explicit revoked/superseded state plus a same-key conflict check at card-write time.

## 6. Memory Reward Inflation in Self-Improving LLM Agents
**Mohammad Asadolahi, Amir Amini, Samira Talebi, Amirfarhad Farhadi, Azadeh Zamanifar** (University of Isfahan) — arXiv preprint (cs.AI), **29 Jun 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00017

**Core mechanism.** Treats each stored episode score as a **proxy reward for an implicit non-parametric policy**, so every retrieval becomes a policy-improvement step whose reliability depends entirely on how that score was produced. In deployment ground truth is absent, so the stored reward is at best an LLM assessment; this substitution produces the **Echo Gap**: incorrect episodes receive inflated rewards and the agent preferentially reuses the mistakes it is most confident in. The missing property is formalized as the **Error-Independence Assumption (EIA)** — a usable verification signal must *both* track truth and **decorrelate its error from the memory bias**; the authors prove EIA is *necessary* for correcting inflation and give the recoverable payoff in closed form. Their answer-free de-inflation algorithm is LUCID.

**Evidence quality.** Medium. The failure mode is demonstrated (agent families and model families) and shown to compound under **plain similarity retrieval**, not only when ranking by stored score — which is the regime a deployed system actually uses. The corrective is validated on BIRD text-to-SQL: 56.9% execution accuracy vs. 54.0% for a Memento-style self-graded agent (+2.9 pts mean across seeds) and 52.4% for an identically-architected memory-less agent. Single domain, modest margins, one algorithm.

**Transfer note.** This is the formal justification for the `hits` / `self_resolved` split you already enforce: a confirming judge whose errors correlate with the original self-assessment cannot detect which memories are overvalued, so verification must come from a source independent of the card's producer — which in your system is the human PR reviewer plus the independent consumer environment, not the source session.

## 7. When Continual Learning Moves to Memory: A Study of Experience Reuse in LLM Agents
**Qisheng Hu, Quanyu Long, Wenya Wang** (Nanyang Technological University) — arXiv preprint (cs.LG), **29 Apr 2026** (marked "working in progress", 1 v1) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27003

**Core mechanism.** **Confirmed to exist** (the ID you supplied is correct). External memory does not dissolve the stability-plasticity dilemma, it **relocates** it: with a bounded context window, old and new experience compete at retrieval, so the bottleneck moves from parameter updates to memory access — interference becomes **retrieval pollution**, context competition, and memory dilution rather than gradient overwrite. The `(k,v)` framework separates *representation* (value: raw trajectory vs. LLM-abstracted **Insight**) from *organization* (key: aggregated task-level bundles vs. individual insight units; retrieved once per task vs. per step), and evaluates with classical continual-learning metrics — Forward Transfer, Backward Transfer, and an RR/NL split by baseline-success vs. baseline-fail subsets.

**Evidence quality.** Medium (controlled study, not a system). Sequential-task experiments in ALFWorld and BabyAI, 200 training instances per phase, frozen backbone with the ReMe memory module. Sharp, quantified findings: in the A→B direction **Raw trajectories produce negative transfer (ALFWorld FWT −9.5; BabyAI −7.5) while Insight is positive (+6.5; +9.0)**; under Raw the damage is concentrated on hard cases (**ALFWorld ΔRR +4.6 but ΔNL −26.1** — the easy subset is preserved while the hard subset degrades sharply); and within-task advantage does **not** predict cross-task benefit (Raw's large within-task edge on ALFWorld-B vanishes or reverses cross-task). Finer storage granularity and more frequent retrieval help or hurt depending on experience diversity and target-task structure; the design with the strongest forward transfer simultaneously induced severe forgetting.

**Transfer note.** Your KB is small and curated rather than a growing pool, which sidesteps their most brutal result (memory dilution), but two findings bite directly: store **abstract insight, not replayable command logs**, since detailed procedural memory is what produces the −26.1 point hard-case collapse; and never validate a new card by its within-task hit rate alone — measure whether it broke already-solved cases.

## 8. Experience Memory Graph: One-Shot Error Correction for Agents
**Wenjun Wang, Yuchen Fang, Fengrui Liu, Zibo Liang, Kai Zheng** — arXiv preprint (cs.AI), v1 15 Jul 2026, v2 10 Aug 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13884

**Core mechanism.** Reformulates agent failure recovery as **graph matching**. At training time both failed exploration trajectories and successful expert trajectories are converted into directed **action decision graphs**; matching them extracts common subgraphs (successful workflows) and **graph edit paths** that state explicitly how to repair a failure — which actions to **add, delete, or relabel** under a given observation — and these are stored in a memory graph with **intra-task nodes and cross-task edges**. At test time the agent retrieves relevant insights and executes in a **single loop-free pass**, with no trial-and-error.

**Evidence quality.** Medium. ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, consistently beating state-of-the-art prompt-reflection baselines on success rate and average reward while requiring no test-time trial-and-error. The stated weakness of the baseline family — prompt-based reflection is brittle, expensive in iterative API calls, and produces task-specific memory that may not generalize — is exactly the critique behind the design. No large-scale multi-model sweep, and the graphs are built from expert trajectories, so the approach assumes a supply of successful exemplars.

**Transfer note.** This is your case schema, formalized: a card's `diagnosis`/`fix_on_mismatch` is a graph edit path (add / delete / relabel an action under an observation), and making that edit set the explicit stored unit — rather than prose steps — is what enables the one-shot, loop-free apply and makes card diffs reviewable in a PR.

## Cross-cutting patterns

Four mechanisms recur across the verified 2026 set and map cleanly onto a curated case store. **Structured delta edits with an explicit identity** are now standard — ACE's id-keyed bullets merged by non-LLM logic, Recuris patching exactly the attributed component, EMG storing add/delete/relabel paths — and the shared motive is that full rewriting erodes detail (ACE's "context collapse") while keeping historical validity auditable (TEPA preserving revoked precedents). **Retrieval is becoming utility- and structure-gated rather than similarity-ranked**: Recuris invokes on an execution event against a working state, APEX-EM runs structural-signature and graph channels alongside semantic search and reports 3.3× the transfer for structure, EMG matches graphs, and Memory Reward Inflation shows that similarity-only retrieval is not even safe from score inflation. **Consolidation, dedup, and pruning are the acknowledged weak spot across the board** — Recuris admits 17 near-duplicates and deprecates nothing, TEPA shows append-only and last-write-wins fall *below no memory at all* under reversal, and ACE needs a semantic-embedding dedup pass whose threshold is non-monotone — so revocation, supersession-by-key, and observed breakage of already-solved cases belong in the write path, not in a periodic cleanup. Finally, **self-reported success is the field's most consistent failure**: ACE's own counters come from the Generator, ReasoningBank's memory is distilled from *self-judged* outcomes, and Memory Reward Inflation proves that a verifier correlated with the original self-assessment cannot recover the inflation — independent verification (human review, an independent consumer environment, or a held-out anchor set) is the only gate that keeps accumulating memory from becoming a liability.

## Appendix — further verified 2026 work, not in the top 8

- **ReasoningBank: Scaling Agent Self-Evolving with Reasoning Memory** — Siru Ouyang, Jun Yan, I-Hung Hsu, … Jiawei Han, Chen-Yu Lee, Tomas Pfister (Google Cloud AI Research) — **ICLR 2026**; arXiv:2509.25140 v1 29 Sep 2025, v2 16 Mar 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140. Distills generalizable reasoning strategies from **self-judged successes and failures**, retrieves by task relevance, integrates new learnings back; adds memory-aware test-time scaling (MaTTS). This is the canonical Google anchor and the paper most often miscited as a 2026 result — its venue is ICLR 2026 but the preprint is Sep 2025, and the memory is distilled from *self-judged* outcomes.
- **STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?** — Hanxiang Chao, Yihan Bai, Rui Sheng, Tianle Li, Yushi Sun — arXiv preprint (cs.CL), **7 May 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527. 400 expert-validated **implicit conflict** scenarios, 1,200 queries, contexts to 150K tokens, probing State Resolution / Premise Resistance / Implicit Policy Adaptation; best evaluated model (Gemini-3.1-pro) reaches only 55.2% overall. The counterintuitive finding worth carrying: 92.0% on detecting a stale state but ~30% on *rejecting a query that presupposes it* — recognizing staleness and acting on it are separable capabilities.
- **MemTrace: Tracing and Attributing Errors in Large Language Model Memory Systems** — Xinle Deng, Ruobin Zhong, …, Ningyu Zhang (Zhejiang University) — arXiv preprint, v1 27 May 2026, v3 16 Jul 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28732. Turns memory pipelines into executable **memory evolution graphs** for operational-level error tracing, with MemTraceBench over Long-Context/RAG/Mem0/EverMemOS; attribution signals then drive prompt optimization in a closed loop, boosting end-task performance up to **7.62%**. This is the misdiagnosis-attribution analogue you asked about, one level below case content.
- **TARSE: Test-Time Adaptation via Retrieval of Skills and Experience for Reasoning Agents** — Junda Wang, Zonghai Tao, Hansi Zeng, Zhichao Yang, Hamed Zamani, Hong Yu (UMass Amherst) — arXiv preprint (cs.IR), **1 Mar 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01241. Separates two explicitly retrievable resources — **skills** (guideline documents organized as executable decision rules) and **experience** (verified reasoning chains indexed by step-level transitions) — and uses a **step-aware retriever** to pick items for the current reasoning step, then adapts on them. Gains over medical-RAG and prompting-only baselines. Directly relevant to your skill-vs-case split and to step-level (not query-level) retrieval timing; no shared same-backbone comparison reported in the abstract.
- **What to Keep, What to Forget: A Rate–Distortion View of Memory Compaction in LLMs and Agents** — Ashwin Gerard Colaco, Nada Lahjouji — arXiv preprint (cs.LG), **9 Jul 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08032. Survey that unifies KV-cache eviction, prompt pruning/distillation, bounded architectural state, and agent-memory consolidation as one rate–distortion decision, with a seven-axis taxonomy. Two findings cut across every layer: the signal deciding what to keep is always **attention magnitude or recency**, and it fails identically everywhere by discarding — before the query is known, irreversibly — the information the query later needs; and repeated compaction, which agents actually perform, is almost never measured.
- **Beyond RAG for Agent Memory: Retrieval by Decoupling and Aggregation (xMemory)** — Zhanghao Hu, Qinglin Zhu, Runcong Zhao, Di Liang, Hanqi Yan, Yulan He, Lin Gui (King's College London / York) — arXiv:2602.02007 v1 2 Feb 2026, v4 12 May 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02007. Argues agent memory is a bounded, coherent stream with many near-duplicate spans, so flat top-k similarity returns redundant context while summary hierarchies blur distinguishing detail; **decouples** reusable facts/updates/distinguishing details first, aggregates into groups under a sparsity–semantic-faithfulness objective, then retrieves **top-down**, expanding to raw messages only when it reduces reader uncertainty. LoCoMo and PerLTQA, multiple open and closed LLMs.
- **Agent Zero Memory: Provenance-Aware Long-Term Memory for LLM Agents** — Ming Wu, Pengyuan Zhu — arXiv preprint (cs.CL), **30 Aug 2026** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29606. Three parallel stores (event timeline, entity-event knowledge graph, citation-locked documentary memory), an intent gate so self-contained turns add no latency, and **hybrid embedding + lexical search** under agent-controlled filters; every learned item carries origin, timestamp, and evidence pointer, and every answer is read under a citation lock so it may cite only evidence the reader actually opened. 95.60% LongMemEval / 93.60% LoCoMo with an accuracy-cost-latency frontier across eight backbones. Most directly usable evidence that **lexical + embedding hybrid retrieval with provenance** is competitive at state of the art.
- **Defeasible Library Learning: Typed Methods with Runtime Contracts that Un-learn on Drift** — Zenodo, v1 29 Jun 2026, v2 5 Jul 2026 — https://zenodo.org/records/21201723. Verified to exist but **not peer-reviewed and not plausibly a primary venue** (Zenodo deposit, no institutional attribution surfaced); included only because its title claims runtime-contract-based un-learning on drift, which is adjacent to TEPA and to your `last_verified` decay. Treat as unverified in quality.
